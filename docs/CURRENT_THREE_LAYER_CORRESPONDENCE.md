## Reading the tables

`M` denotes `manuscript/Update_branch.tex`; `S1` through `S11` are stable sections of the current statement document; `F` denotes the linked Lean source. Each table assesses both adjacent comparisons. The judgment `一致` means that the displayed hypotheses, quantifiers and conclusion agree, allowing a formally stronger result that implies the stated claim. Such strengthening is described in the last column. It does not mean that every intermediate step of the handwritten proof has been formalized.

All manuscript locators below refer to this release's TeX source. Lean links identify source declarations; elaborated types and recursive axiom output must be read in the bound execution records. In particular, `Subgroup.closure` in the cited Lean code is the algebraically generated subgroup. `topologicalClosure` is the separate topological operation.

## Reverse implication and the limits of proof correspondence

For Theorem 1, the formal native and arbitrary-carrier conclusions are the finite projective image and its nondensity under the manuscript's exact encoding and detection assumptions. They directly imply the manuscript statement. A generated subgroup is not substituted for the budgeted image.

For Theorem 2(1), the closed circle subgroup is a stronger witness than the nontrivial continuous one-parameter subgroup requested by the manuscript. Its containment is in the onset-budget image itself. For Theorem 2(2), the final equality theorem derives the local-curve and open-subgroup ingredients internally. The manuscript uses a smooth semialgebraic stratum; the formal proof extracts a local curve from real-algebraic data and uses a local flow. Agreement of the final theorem does not certify those handwritten intermediate steps individually.

For Theorem 3, the original-list interfaces and metric bridges make the formal inequalities apply to the same physical noise positions and recovery class as the manuscript. The lower-saturation witness has exact depth `h`; the upper-saturation result needs only depth at most `h`. The stronger all-positive-laws conclusions imply the stated independent-noise assertions. The local-stochastic estimate is a separate one-sided guarantee and is not used to extend the saturation quantifiers.

The CSS handwritten existence proof samples subspaces and uses Gaussian-binomial estimates. The Lean proof uses finite generator samples and different numerical bounds. Both establish the required simultaneous check-space properties. Their intermediate estimates are not claimed to be line-for-line identical. The bound `t ≥ 20` is sufficient, not a claim that smaller `t` cannot work.

## Non-vacuity and boundary cases

The following are actual source-level instances or constructions, with their scope stated explicitly:

- [`CriticalDepth.ReviewV2.Examples.fiveQubit_native_depthOne` and `fiveQubit_native_depthTwo`](../mathlib-review/CriticalDepthMathlib.lean#L11565) use an explicit isometric five-qubit encoding, arbitrary-operator detection below three, arity two and depths one and two. Thus the nonempty subthreshold range of Theorem 1 is represented by concrete calls.
- [`CriticalDepth.ReviewV2.StandardExamples.fourQubit_standard_critical_circle`](../mathlib-review/CriticalDepthMathlib.lean#L11948) uses the actual four-qubit stabilizer encoding and distance two. The onset is one, consistent with the empty positive-depth range of Theorem 1 when `ρ ≥ d`.
- The Shor saturation theorem constructs the code, its stabilizer property, its actual distance and its gate-list witness together. The CSS theorem constructs the entire check-space conjunction and one code for the full depth interval. A conditional lemma about check spaces alone is not being used as proof that such spaces exist.
- At fault order zero the only history is the no-fault history, and its encoded branch is an isometry. [`CriticalDepth.FaultFirstBadOrder.correctableThrough_zero`](../mathlib-review/FaultFirstBadOrder.lean#L43) rules out a spurious first failure at zero.
- Positive conditional laws exist on each nonempty gate support, for example the uniform distribution on its nonidentity Pauli labels. Empty-support gates supply no noisy location. The zero-layer circuit is allowed and has no fault positions; it does not force the per-code worst order to be finite.

These facts do not assert the existence of a stabilizer code for every arbitrary choice of `n,k,d`. The universal results are conditional on an encoding satisfying the stated hypotheses. No acceptance of an old compiled example is transferred to a changed source merely because its name is unchanged.
