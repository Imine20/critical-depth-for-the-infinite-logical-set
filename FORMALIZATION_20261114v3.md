## Proof obligations

- Theorem 2(2) uses the real coordinate ring modulo the ideal of the actual real circuit points. A real projective coordinate is transcendental on a minimal component, and a derivation gives a polynomial vector field after clearing denominators. Local ODE uniqueness preserves the component and yields a nonzero coordinate derivative. In the unitary cover, conjugates of a nonscalar skew-Hermitian tangent and the scalar phase span the skew-Hermitians. Apply the surjective inverse function theorem in the ambient matrix space with Hermitian correction variables. Local unitarity forces the correction to vanish. The open projective quotient carries the neighborhood to the dense generated subgroup.
- The checked two-history ambiguity estimate uses the Hilbert–Schmidt projection onto the orthogonal logical directions of the two recovered branches. The same end-to-end finite history model supplies the distance-based lower envelope and obtains the upper envelope from a noiseless identity circuit with actual midpoint gate locations. The universal exponent theorem uses the identity circuit in place of a circuit with ideal action equal to the full logical Pauli. The two faulted branches still differ by that logical Pauli.
- In Q37, the phase certificates can be expressed as degree at most three Boolean multilinear polynomials modulo eight for the logical (T) layer, and degree at most two modulo four for the square-root layer. The distance certificate can search for collisions of syndrome and different logical labels among masks of weight at most three. This is equivalent to the absence of a nontrivial logical mask of weight at most six. See [Dua et al., Appendix B](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06730v1#A2).
